\begin{abstract}
Mobile payment platforms face escalating financial risks including organized fraud, money laundering, and account theft.
Traditional rule-based risk control systems suffer from slow iteration, limited coverage, and high operational costs.
We propose \textbf{RiskPilot}, an autonomous evolution engine that forms a closed-loop pipeline:
(1)~a \textbf{GNN perception layer} based on Beta Wavelet Graph Neural Networks (BWGNN) that detects anomalous nodes and extracts structured risk insights from transaction graphs;
(2)~an \textbf{LLM strategy layer} that automatically generates and optimizes executable risk control rules via retrieval-augmented generation (RAG);
(3)~a \textbf{sandbox validation layer} that backtests rules on labeled datasets with recall, precision, and false positive rate metrics;
(4)~a \textbf{rule knowledge base} that accumulates qualified rules for iterative refinement.
Experiments on the T-Finance dataset (39K nodes, 42M edges) demonstrate that BWGNN identifies 5 distinct fraud patterns with near-perfect anomaly scores, and the closed-loop mechanism enables continuous rule quality improvement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

% 1.1 背景与挑战
Mobile payment has become an indispensable part of daily life, with platforms like WeChat Pay serving billions of transactions.
However, the convenience and scale of mobile payment also attract increasingly sophisticated financial risks:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Accelerating risk evolution}: Fraud methods are upgrading toward intelligent, organized, and covert patterns, outpacing traditional rule-based defenses.
  \item \textbf{Diverse and long-tailed risk patterns}: Effective detection requires integrating multi-modal data including text, user profiles, behavioral sequences, and transaction networks.
  \item \textbf{Operational inefficiency}: Manual case-by-case review is costly, slow, and degrades user experience.
\end{itemize}

% 1.2 动机
Existing graph-based anomaly detection methods~\cite{tang2022rethinking} can effectively identify suspicious nodes, but their numerical outputs (anomaly scores, embeddings) cannot be directly translated into actionable risk control rules.
Meanwhile, Large Language Models (LLMs) excel at generating structured text but lack the ability to perceive graph-structural risk patterns.

% 1.3 贡献
We propose \textbf{RiskPilot}, which bridges this gap through a four-stage closed-loop pipeline.
Our main contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A novel \textbf{GNN-to-LLM insight bridge} that converts GNN numerical outputs into structured risk descriptions consumable by LLMs (Section~\ref{sec:insight}).
  \item An \textbf{autonomous rule evolution loop}: GNN perception $\rightarrow$ LLM rule generation $\rightarrow$ sandbox validation $\rightarrow$ knowledge base accumulation $\rightarrow$ feedback optimization.
  \item Experimental validation on the T-Finance dataset showing the effectiveness of spectral-based anomaly detection and the feasibility of automated rule generation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Graph-Based Anomaly Detection}
% GNN 异常检测综述: GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE, 频谱方法
Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) have been widely applied to fraud detection in financial networks~\cite{tang2022rethinking}.
Most GNN-based methods operate in the spatial domain (e.g., GCN, GAT, GraphSAGE), aggregating neighbor information iteratively.
However, Tang et al.~\cite{tang2022rethinking} revealed that anomalous nodes exhibit distinct \textit{spectral} characteristics—they tend to have higher energy in specific frequency bands.
Their proposed BWGNN uses Beta wavelet bases to decompose graph signals at multiple scales, achieving state-of-the-art performance on financial anomaly detection benchmarks.

\subsection{LLM for Risk Control}
% LLM 在风控中的应用
Recent works have explored using LLMs for security and risk analysis, including vulnerability detection, threat intelligence summarization, and policy generation.
However, applying LLMs to \textit{graph-structural} risk pattern understanding and \textit{executable rule generation} remains largely unexplored.

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation}
% RAG 技术
RAG~\cite{lewis2020retrieval} enhances LLM generation quality by retrieving relevant context from external knowledge bases.
We adopt RAG to supply the LLM with historically effective rules during rule generation and optimization.

% ---- Stage 1 ----
\subsection{Stage 1: GNN Perception Layer}
\label{sec:insight}

\subsubsection{Beta Wavelet GNN (BWGNN)}
We adopt BWGNN~\cite{tang2022rethinking} as the backbone anomaly detector.
Given a graph $G=(V,E)$ with node features $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times F}$ and normalized Laplacian $\hat{L}$, BWGNN constructs $d+1$ polynomial filter bases using Beta wavelets:
\begin{equation}
  \psi_i(\lambda) = \frac{(\lambda/2)^i (1-\lambda/2)^{d-i}}{B(i+1, d+1-i)}, \quad i=0,\ldots,d
\end{equation}
where $B(\cdot,\cdot)$ is the Beta function.
Each basis captures a different frequency band, enabling multi-scale spectral analysis.

The model outputs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Anomaly probabilities: $\hat{y} = \text{softmax}(f_\theta(X, G)) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 2}$
  \item Node embeddings: $Z = g_\theta(X, G) \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times H}$ (penultimate layer)
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Risk Insight Extraction (Key Innovation)}
We bridge GNN outputs to LLM inputs through structured insight extraction:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Anomaly node selection}: Select top-$K$ nodes by anomaly score above threshold $\tau$.
  \item \textbf{Embedding clustering}: Apply KMeans on anomaly node embeddings to discover $C$ distinct risk patterns.
  \item \textbf{Structural analysis}: For each cluster, compute degree statistics, local subgraph density, and feature z-scores.
  \item \textbf{Natural language description}: Generate human-readable risk descriptions for LLM consumption.
\end{enumerate}

% ---- Stage 2 ----
\subsection{Stage 2: LLM Strategy Layer}

The LLM rule generator takes risk insights and historical rules (via RAG) as input, and outputs structured JSON rules:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Each rule contains: conditions (feature thresholds), logical operators (AND/OR), severity level, and action (block/review/alert).
  \item A system prompt constrains the LLM to follow risk control best practices.
  \item Failed rules from sandbox validation are fed back for iterative optimization.
\end{itemize}

% ---- Stage 3 ----
\subsection{Stage 3: Sandbox Validation Layer}

Rules are parsed into executable Python functions and backtested on the labeled dataset:
\begin{equation}
  \text{Recall} = \frac{TP}{TP+FN}, \quad
  \text{FPR} = \frac{FP}{FP+TN}, \quad
  \text{F1} = \frac{2 \cdot P \cdot R}{P + R}
\end{equation}
A rule is qualified if: $\text{F1} \geq 0.6$, $\text{Recall} \geq 0.5$, $\text{FPR} \leq 0.05$.

% ---- Stage 4 ----
\subsection{Stage 4: Rule Knowledge Base}

Qualified rules are vectorized and stored in a ChromaDB collection for RAG retrieval in subsequent iterations, forming the closed-loop feedback mechanism.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Strengths}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The GNN-to-LLM insight bridge enables structural risk patterns to be consumed by language models.
  \item The closed-loop design ensures continuous rule quality improvement without manual intervention.
  \item Beta wavelet spectral analysis captures anomaly patterns that spatial GNNs miss.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations and Future Work}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Current results use mock LLM rules; real LLM integration is needed for full validation.
  \item The insight extractor relies on KMeans clustering, which assumes spherical clusters—density-based methods (e.g., DBSCAN) may better capture irregular fraud patterns.
  \item Temporal dynamics (transaction sequences over time) are not yet modeled.
  \item Scalability to T-Social (5.7M nodes) requires mini-batch training strategies.
\end{itemize}


% ============================================================
% 6. Conclusion
% ============================================================
\section{Conclusion}

We presented RiskPilot, an autonomous evolution engine for financial risk control rules.
By bridging GNN anomaly detection with LLM rule generation through structured risk insights, and closing the loop via sandbox validation and knowledge base accumulation, RiskPilot enables risk control systems to become smarter over time.
Experiments on T-Finance demonstrate the effectiveness of spectral-based anomaly detection and the feasibility of the proposed pipeline architecture.
